# Changelog

## 0.9.0 (2026-11-23)

First public beta: every feature is in.

### The plate

- A plate along the bottom of the screen that follows Blizzard's action bars, stance, pet and totem bars, the micro menu, the bags, the chat, the damage meter and the minimap where Edit Mode puts them, and draws round them: its reach, its corners, its margins and its recesses are settings. Static outside Edit Mode.
- A border in KaLi's bronze or another metal, faceted and polished like Blizzard's own rings, and a background in one of the game's materials.
- Nine styles, each a minimap ring and a color: Blizzard copper (the default), silver, gold, forest green, rune taupe, steampunk bronze, gem pink and gold, astronomy blue, fire red. A style sets the ring, the border, the background, the windows' cartouche and the corners at once, nothing else; one that would replace a setting you changed yourself asks first, naming it.
- With the plate turned off, the info bar keeps its own background.

### The info bar

- An info bar at the plate's foot, with its own items (zone, clock, bags, gold, repairs, experience, performance, speed, volume) and other addons' LibDataBroker items. A new addon's readout lands in the **Addons** folder, never on the bar.
- The padlock, a metal block closing the bar at its right end, decides what the mouse does: shut, the HUD is used; open, the block turns gold with a soft aura, and each element is dragged along the bar, round the minimap or into a folder. A fight that starts while it's open shuts it, and it is shut again at every connection and reload.
- One right-click: on any of ours or Blizzard's minimap buttons, its settings and the folder it goes into. Another addon's item keeps its own right-click until the padlock is open, as in Edit Mode; one with no click of its own opens its settings.
- Nothing on the bar moves by itself, nor lies over another. Each item keeps a place as wide as the widest it has shown, so a text that changes shifts nothing. What has no room on the bar (a large text, a narrow screen, many addons) goes into **More**, a folder that shows only while it holds something; it is back on the bar as soon as it fits, and any folder can take it instead.
- The bar's menu lists what it can show, by category (the folders, not each macro), then the padlock, Edit Mode and what can be made there (a folder, a macro, a note, where you opened the menu), and undo and redo last.
- Undo and redo for the bar's last ten changes (a move, a copy, a folder, macro or separator made or deleted, a rename, an item's reset), from the bar's menu or two keys to bind.
- Any item with an icon shows its icon and its text, its icon alone or its text alone, from its menu.
- Every element can be taken off the HUD from its menu (**Remove**): what isn't KaLi HUD's to destroy, and the folders KaLi HUD made, with what they hold; the bar's menu puts them back where they were. Your own folders, macros and separators are deleted (a folder asks first, as what it holds goes with it). One of KaLi HUD's items can be reset: its own settings and its place.
- What is shown again from the bar's menu, sent into a folder or brought back by an undo flashes once where it lands.
- The tooltips show the mouse's left and right buttons as icons, the house editor's, left first, then right, and the element's type on the last line. A tooltip goes once its click opens something (a pane, a folder's list, a menu, a note, another addon's window, the calendar), and none shows over an open folder; the memory's and the sound's, whose click opens nothing, stay, brought up to date.
- The clock follows the game's own settings (12 or 24 hours, local or realm time).
- The zone, its coordinates, PvP state and levels open the map your map key opens, another addon's when it took that key.
- The zone's PvP state says whose territory it is where the game names no faction (a PvE realm): yours where it is friendly, the other faction's where it is hostile.
- The zone's levels, on the bar and under fishing's tooltip, are Forever's own Group Finder's; a city or a zone it doesn't list shows none.
- Fishing's tooltip says where a cast lands here: from the zone's base skill, every cast from base + 95 (Westfall 1-75), green once every one does, red while none can; a dungeon's and a sub-zone's own (Azshara's Bay of Storms) too. The bases are the original game's; where none is known (Forever's own zones), a question mark.
- A skill's bonus says where it comes from: the fishing rod, a lure, the gloves' enchantment, a buff, a set, a talent, the race; a source whose amount the game doesn't give wears a question mark, and the tooltip says what it means.
- **New measure**, in the session item's menu: gold, experience, deaths and time counted from now.

### Folders

- Folders hold what is dragged into them and gather other addons' minimap buttons, one made by an addon loaded later too. With the info bar off, those buttons go back on the minimap, as does a minimap button whose addon also has an item on the bar.
- A folder made from the bar's menu appears where you opened that menu; one made from an element's menu takes its place, the element inside. A click outside an open folder shuts it. A folder with its name on its button has a tooltip too.
- A folder's **List** sets its list: icons alone, icons and text, or text alone; its values aligned on the list's right, and those that say nothing (0, 0%, "None") in gray; its flow; its items per line, or per column when it runs down (a slider, 1 to 25); the way it opens and how.
- A list of texts reads from the top left whichever way it opens, so a section's title stands over its items.
- A separator can be named, from its right-click: in a list with text running down, its name is a section's title, in gold over its rule. It can also start the next column, or the next row in a list running across, whatever the count.
- Ready-made folders: the attributes', with the character sheet's titles in three columns, its values aligned, the empty ones gray; the emotes' and the group's markers, with titles and a column of their own; the statistics, reputations, skills and reagents, their values aligned.
- The ready-made folders are locked folders (their type says "Locked folder"): KaLi HUD keeps what they hold, made again at each login, so a new version's contents simply appear, and their names, headings and emotes follow the game's language when you switch it (**Addons**' and **More**'s names too). Nothing goes into one, nothing in it changes, and there is no reset. Drag something out of one, the padlock open, and a copy lands where you drop it; the folder keeps its own. A copy of an item shows the same value and is deleted from its own menu; a copied macro is yours to edit. A locked folder is taken off the HUD whole from its menu and comes back whole from the bar's menu. **Addons** and **More** are not locked: they hold what lands in them.
- A **Reputation** folder: each faction of the reputation pane with its standing; its menu shows a faction on the progress bar.
- The group's folder: its markers put their sign on your target with a left click and on the ground with a right click (the game's targeting circle), and **Clear All** clears both; the loot rules are set from their item by the group's leader, the method and the threshold, as from the game's portrait.

### Macros and notes

- Macro buttons, on the bar or in a folder. A macro can have a right-click of its own, its own commands run while the padlock is shut, and a tooltip of its own in place of its commands.
- The macro editor has a section for each button: its icon and the count of its letters at its head, its short description (said in the macro's tooltip by the button's icon), then its commands; the right button's help says when they run; empty boxes say in gray what goes in them.
- A note opens with a left click and shuts with a second; its editor has no **Runs** row.
- A macro's or a note's editor opens next to its button, as a folder's list does; the icon library shuts once an icon is picked.

### The character sheet's attributes

- Each attribute on the bar, its hover a card of its own: its name and its value large, then where the value comes from (its base; all the gear gives under **Gear**, an item's "Equip:" bonuses and its enchantment with it; the sets' bonuses under **Item Sets**; the rest under **Bonus**; each group with its sum), what there is to know of it in the character sheet's own words, and what it gives, as figures ("+220 Attack Power"), with what a point of each attribute that raises it gives ("Strength: 2 Attack Power per point"). A percent of health (the tauren's Endurance), of armor (a druid's bear form, Toughness) or of a resistance is worked out, what it comes to said, and what's left with it; the mana's regeneration says what it can't name. The health's regeneration has a card of its own beside the mana's: in 5 sec in a fight, out of one in brackets, made of what your spirit gives, items' and buffs' health every 5 sec (Demon Skin, Troll's Blood elixirs) and what changes it by a share (Pure Water, Rage). The spell power has a value for each school, holy to arcane, each with the character sheet's icon for it under **Spell Power**, as the resistances have theirs: each card names that school's sources, an item's spell power counted on all six, and a warlock's pet's share sits on the school it's taken from. A weapon has its speed, its damage a second and its skill ("Swords: 120 (5)/150"), the haste what it is, the defense where its "/ 115" comes from, the skill's most at your level. A soft green light along the card's border beside where the value comes from, a blue one beside what it gives, and what its clicks do on one line at its foot.
- Every source named, each by how much: each worn item and its enchantment ("Enchantment (Wrist)", by its slot), a weapon's oil or stone, a set's bonus, each buff, debuff or elixir, each talent at its rank, the race's and the class's passive spells, and what's left. Each base is named after where it comes from, as the game's tooltip names a character: your class's own chance ("Shaman 1.7%" of critical strike), its health at your level ("Shaman level 24"), the mana by your class at level 1 and what the levels since add, the game's own table, with **Others** for what it can't name ("Shaman 55", "Levels 2 to 24 (476 - 55) 421"), an attribute by your race, your class and what levels 2 to yours have added, the game's base less the other two ("Orc 23", "Shaman 2", "Levels 2 to 24 (40 - 23 - 2) 15" of spirit: each class gains each attribute at its own pace), the defense's "Skill Level", the attack power's level and how ("Level 24 (2 x 24 - 20)"), each shown as a source, as the attributes are. What the card works out from what the game doesn't give wears the question mark, its foot saying how: the race's and the class's figures at level 1 (the original game's, which Forever's files don't hold), and a base that is what the game's figure leaves (the health's, a chance's); the armor and the resistances have no base, what the card can't name of them being **Others**; one nothing gives says "None". Each item, buff, talent and passive spell has its icon, an item under **Gear** even alone. The sets' group also says, in gray, what the next pieces of a set you wear would give ("Battlegear (2/3) +40").
- Counted as the game counts: a talent or a passive spell that needs a weapon or a form counts only with it; the hit chance, the critical strike and the haste name the sources of the kind of attack they show (melee, ranged or spells), so a buff of melee and ranged haste counts once; a percent buff counts on its own attribute, and Blessing of Kings leaves no false **Others**; of two speed raises the strongest alone counts, the other said "doesn't stack", and a mount's or a swim's speed is read; the dodge, the parry and the block name the defense either way of its most ("-0.08%" at 113 / 115), the critical strike the weapon skill; a figure that grows with the level (a druid's forms, Rockbiter, Battle Shout) is worked out at yours; a rating you don't have yet is what the game makes of it at your level. Spiritual Guidance, Blood Fury, the expertise, the armor ignored and the energy are named; so is the attack power a shaman's Mental Dexterity or a hunter's Careful Aim gives from the intellect, a quiver's ranged speed, Juju Flurry's and Seal of the Crusader's melee speed, the gnome's Expansive Mind and debuffs on the armor from your gear. The attack power's level part is what your level and class give, always the game's, and a class that shoots no ranged weapon has no ranged attack power (a shaman's 0, not -10 and Mental Dexterity's); what the card can't account for of the game's base shows in red as **Addon mistake**, kept for the bug report. A talent's value is the one its own tooltip states at your rank.
- A left click keeps the card open: it stays while you change an item, take a buff or pick a target, and follows; a drag moves it, its cross, Escape or a click on the attribute again shuts it. Several can be kept open, each opening over the list it was clicked in, the next above it, then in the next column to the right, then to the left; one shut leaves its place, the others staying. On a card kept open, each source's row shows on hover the game's own tooltip of what it names (the item worn, the buff with its time left, the talent at its rank, the spell), or how the card works its figure out. While one is open, an item, a talent, a consumable or a meal under the pointer is tried on it: the card says in light blue what the value would be and which of its rows would come, go or change (an item's own stats, its enchantment, its set, what its attributes give; an elixir's or a meal's buff, its figure the game's data's); what can't be worked out wears the question mark.
- What is worth reaching, and whether it is reached or what is missing, first against what you fight now (your target when it is an enemy of your level, one or two above, else an enemy of your level), then against a raid boss: never missing it (a raid boss: 8% or 9% with weapons, the game giving both, 17% with spells, 27% or 28% with two weapons; one or two levels above: 5.2% or 5.5%, 5.4% or 6%), none of its critical strikes (440 defense against a raid boss at level 60), never dodged by it (6.5% expertise) and, against one of your level, its parry (5%), the armor's 75% and the armor short of it, a resistance's 75% on average. A weapon skill short of its most adds what Forever's Skills tab says to the thresholds, said under them.
- An attribute with thresholds (hit, defense, expertise, armor, resistances) wears after its value, in gold, the highest level of enemy it reaches, I to IV: an enemy of your level, one and two levels above, a raid boss. Its card lists each level, what each threshold is and whether it's reached or what's missing, then how the figures are had and why they're worth reaching. The defense's card says, level by level, how much of the enemy's blows you avoid or block (its misses, your dodge, parry and block), and where it can land crushing blows (three levels above: 15% of its blows, at 150% of the damage), how many land and what leaves them no room. The critical strike's card, when it shows the melee's, says each level's glancing blows (how often, and by how much less damage, as the game gives them) and the white hits' ceiling: past it, a critical strike more is lost on your automatic attacks (two figures, the game's and the original game's, a question mark between them).
- A question mark stands only where the game leaves a question open, and the card's foot says what each asks and what to go by: a raid boss's hit with weapons (8% or 9%) and its parry (14% or 16.5%), the game giving both, aim for the higher; a resistance's 75%; a source whose amount follows its caster's power or its item's level.
- The camp's benefits (the fish bowl, the lute, the sharpening wheel…) each have a line of their own, "Camp:" and the item's name with its icon, instead of **Others**, at the figure the game gives at your level. The game hides them from addons, so the card reads them from the buff's own text. In a fight they may stay in **Others**.
- How an attribute's thresholds are worked out and why they're worth reaching take one line on its card, **How, why…**: on the card kept open with a left click, its hover shows them in a tooltip.
- The mana's and the health's regeneration are tables, every figure per 5 sec: a column for each state, the mana out of casting and while casting (the 5 sec after spending mana, when the spirit's stops), the health standing, seated or lying down, and in a fight, with each column's total at the foot. What gives mana or health by itself every few seconds, which the game's own figures don't count, comes under **On top of the game's figure**: Blessing of Wisdom, Mana Spring and Mana Tide Totems, the camp's well, the fish and soup buffs that give mana, a HoT, eating (seated only), each with its own rhythm under it when it isn't 5 sec ("4 every 2 sec"). Meditation, Mage Armor and the like show only while casting, with what they keep; Innervate's two effects each in its own column. The line and the card's head show everything the character gets, casting (or in a fight) first, the game's own figure under the head; the seated spirit wears the question mark (33% more by the game's text, 50% on the original server). A meal or a potion tried on a card kept open shows what it would add. A setting shows the mana per second.
- The block value has a card of its own after the block: the shield's own (as its tooltip writes it), the strength's part (one point in 20, as the character sheet's strength says), the point the game takes off both ("Base -1"), items' and buffs' block value, what's left; what a block takes off a blow.
- A part the card works out of a skill writes its sum: "Defense (118 - 115) x 0.04%" on the dodge, parry and block, "Skill (Swords) (113 - 115) x 0.04%" on the critical strike.
- The spell damage and the resistances share the last column, in one order (arcane, fire, frost, nature, shadow, then holy for the spell damage) and with the same icons.
- The defense's column has four more rows, against an enemy of your level, each card writing its sum: **Damage Reduction** (the armor's, the game's own figure), **Missed**, **Avoidance** (missed, dodged, parried and blocked together) and **Crit Taken**.
- Each attribute's base is split by the original game's table of every race and class at levels 1 to 60: the race's, what the class adds at level 1, what the levels since have given, and **Forever tweaks** for whatever the game's base differs from that table by (Forever's shaman grows a little differently). The two Skyborne races each have their own race figures, read from Forever's level 1 character sheets. The mana's base is one row, the class's at your level from Forever's own table.
- The spirit's figures are cut to a whole everywhere, as the character sheet writes them, so its card and the regenerations' always agree. A hit or critical strike source on some schools only (a few holy ones) says which, and leaves nothing said beside it.
- The character sheet's French texts that put a paragraph's full stop at the start of the next one (the spells' power and healing) have it back at the end of their line.
- A card that counts more than the game gives says so: what's left below 0 is **Addon mistake** in red with its question mark, not **Others**. A card that can't account for all of the game's figure keeps what it read (the game's figures; your level, race, class and form; each aura, item, enchantment and talent, with what it counted) for the bug report, the same card once. `/kh stats` keeps every card as it is, to report from a camp, after a level-up or in a form; `/kh clear` empties them with the errors. Forty are kept: the last `/kh stats` whole, then the cards that couldn't account for their figure, the newest first. Nothing is kept in a fight, and never a character's or a realm's name.

### The minimap

- The minimap in a dome, with a choice of rings, Blizzard's own buttons round it or on the bar. Among the rings: fire, dark iron with lava in it; lava, dark rock with glowing cracks; steampunk. The game's own coordinates under the map are switched off while KaLi HUD has it, and given back as you had them, at each logout too: a game started without KaLi HUD shows them.

### The chat and the damage meter

- The chat and the damage meter fold into a cartouche on the plate, with a choice of cartouches, an animation (on or off) and a sound; folded, the cartouche lies behind the info bar's buttons, its foot fading out down to the bar's middle. Each cartouche is half its window's width by default (**Cartouche width**, as a share of the window).
- The Steampunk, Silver, Golden, Astronomy, Gem, Fire and Rune styles have their own cartouche: the Steampunk's an octagon between lit tubes and gauges, the Rune's a hexagon, the Golden's a shield over its ring, the Astronomy's a twelve-sided ring with the zodiac's signs, the Fire's and the Gem's made to match their rings.
- Typing unfolds the folded chat from any tab docked with it (the game's **IM Style** chat style).

### The progress bars

- Two progress bars: experience, the watched reputation or honor, each with its end cap, its fill, its text and its place.
- The experience bar's tooltip lists what raises the experience you gain now, each by its share, from monsters or quests where they differ, and their sum: being rested, the sleeping bag's rested buff, a meal's bonus (which the game hides, read from the meal's buff), an elixir, an event's buff. The game doesn't say how they add up, so the sum wears the question mark. The camp's tents add to your rest, not to what you gain.
- The reputation bar's tooltip lists what raises the reputation you gain with that faction, and their sum: the human's Diplomacy (+10%, every faction), Cenarion Favor (+25%, the Cenarion Circle only).

### Settings

- In Edit Mode, one dialog for all of it, opened from the KaLi HUD strip along the foot of the screen, with **Revert Changes** and **Defaults** (which asks first), and each section its own **Revert** and **Defaults** on one line under it. Its list of looks is **Style**.
- A page of its own in the game's options, the addon compartment, and `/kh`, with two pages under it: **Presets** and **Behavior & Sounds**. What the HUD does by itself and what it sounds like is set there (the minimap's auto zoom-out, the chat shown while typing, the meter unfolded in a fight, folders shut in a fight, other experience bars hidden, the mana's regeneration per second, the folds' animation and sounds, each sound with a button that plays it); Edit Mode's dialog keeps what is set while looking at it, and which parts of the HUD are on is in both. What an item has of its own (the bags' free slots, the gold's copper) is in its right-click.
- **Presets** starts with the settings in use: this character's own or not, saved as a preset, shared. Your settings are the account's, the same for every character, new ones too. A character can have its own (**This character's own**, as the game's key bindings): it starts from a copy and no longer follows the others; another character can take them from the presets' list. Back on the account's, which asks first, its own are kept as a preset of yours.
- Presets: all you have set in KaLi HUD kept under a name, then a dropdown to pick one and its buttons (**Apply…**, **Update**, **Rename**, **Share…**, **Delete**), each grayed while the one picked doesn't allow it. **Apply…** opens it: a box for each part (the look; the plate, margins and minimap; the progress bars; the info bar with its folders, macros and notes; behavior and sounds), all ticked, then **Apply**; what is unticked stays as you have it, and your settings as they were are kept in the list, to go back to. A preset is shared as a text another player imports: the import adds a preset and changes nothing until you apply it, and says when it holds macros, whose commands run on a click. `/kh export` and `/kh import` do the same; the text opens selected, and Ctrl+C copies it and shuts the window. The chat folded and the padlock open are no part of a preset.
- Nothing of yours is reset, replaced or dropped without a question: the options page's **Reset**, an element's reset, a character going back to the account's settings, and a macro's or a note's editor shut with something typed and not saved, which offers to save it.

### Reliability

- A bug report, from the options page (**Help**) or `/kh report`: a link that opens a new issue, then the report to paste into it, both selected by a click for Ctrl+C. It carries the addon's state, what the attributes' cards couldn't account for, and a check at login that the game still has all the addon uses and answers as it assumes. When it doesn't, a line in chat says so, once per game version, and the report keeps what it was.
- Sturdier when something unexpected happens: another addon's button or one of Blizzard's that can't be placed is left out and the bar laid out without it; a change that fails half way is put back whole; a part of the HUD that didn't load is named, and the rest starts; an error in a click or a menu is kept for the report. The status and the report say when the saved settings had to be repaired.
- In French, English, German and Spanish (Spain's, and Mexico's with the game's own Mexican words); other clients get English.
- The languages reviewed: German speaks to the player as the game (Euch, Euer), Spanish too (tú); the attributes' card's "How" and "Why" rewritten in all four; the game's words (Éraflures, Streifschläge, Bei Mouseover, Abzeichen); each language its own typography (French's no-break spaces before : ; ? !, its ’ and « », English “ ”, German „ “, Spanish « » and ¿ ¡); one language a file under `Locale/`; the chat lines' colon in the player's language; the mouse buttons as the house editor's icons in the hints and the tips.
- The folder icons are named in German and Spanish too, Mexico's with its own words; Castilian writes thousands as the RAE (1500, 12 500).
- A minimap crowded with another addon's pins no longer causes a "stack overflow" error: past a few thousand, its buttons are looked for one by one.
